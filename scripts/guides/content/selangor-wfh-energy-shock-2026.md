## The headline

:::stat
value: Tue–Thu only
label: Selangor state civil servants now in office; Mon &amp; Fri are WFH days
note: The state government framed it as crisis-mode response to the 2026 energy shock. Private sector employers are being urged to mirror it.
:::

The maths is direct. Two WFH days a week is **40% fewer commutes**. For a typical Klang Valley commuter spending RM 350–600 a month on petrol and tolls, that's RM 140–240 a month back — before you touch electricity, food, or wear-and-tear.

## Where the savings actually come from

:::steps
title: Fuel + tolls (the obvious one)
text: Two fewer commute days a week. If you log every fill-up under a `Petrol` category in Duitful, the May vs April delta will be the cleanest measurement of your WFH dividend. Toll receipts go under `Toll` — same logic.
---
title: Electricity at home (the offsetting one)
text: WFH means more aircon, more lights, more appliances at home. Track this monthly under `Electricity`. Net savings = fuel + toll savings minus the electricity bump. If the bump is bigger than expected, switch off appliances during the office days, not the WFH days.
---
title: Lunch &amp; coffee (the silent one)
text: Office days creep with kopitiam lunches, ice blends, tapau dinners. WFH days are usually cheaper food. Tag these as `Food · Out` vs `Food · Home` in Duitful — most people are shocked by the gap once they see it as a chart.
---
title: Vehicle maintenance (the slow one)
text: Less mileage means longer service intervals, fewer tyre changes, less wear on the clutch and brakes. Don't expect this in month one. Expect it in month nine when your service appointment doesn't roll around as fast.
:::

## For SMEs: the calculation is different

For an SME owner with a small office in PJ, Shah Alam, or Subang, the WFH switch is a four-line P&amp;L change:

:::compare
title: What goes down
- Office electricity (lights, aircon, server room)
- Pantry &amp; cleaning supplies
- Reimbursable parking &amp; fuel claims
- Wear on shared office assets
---
title: What goes up (or stays)
- Cloud storage &amp; SaaS seats (already paid)
- Internet (most teams already on fibre at home)
- One-off ergonomics stipend (chair, monitor) — capex, not opex
- Coordination overhead (offset by Tue–Thu in-office anchor days)
:::

If you keep all four under separate Categories in Duitful and tag them `WFH` in the description, the monthly Report shows you the net effect after the dust settles. Most Malaysian SMEs we've seen run the maths land at **RM 800–RM 2,500 net savings per employee per month** once the office is downsized or sublet.

## What to ignore

:::steps
title: "We'll save 50% on everything" pitches
text: WFH cuts commute-related and small office overhead costs. It does not cut your housing, your loan repayments, your insurance, or your kids' school fees. Realistic full-household savings sit at 5–12% of monthly outflow. Anyone quoting bigger numbers is selling you something.
---
title: Buying expensive WFH gear immediately
text: A standing desk and ergonomic chair pay back if you'll WFH long-term. They don't pay back if the policy reverses in six months. Wait one full quarter before any single purchase above RM 800.
---
title: Cancelling office leases mid-term
text: For SMEs: do the WFH net-savings calculation across a full quarter before you renegotiate or break a lease. Lease break fees and the cost of re-leasing later usually wipe out the first 6–12 months of savings. Sublet first, downsize at renewal.
:::

## Common questions

:::faq
q: Is the Selangor WFH order legally enforceable on the private sector?
a: No. The state's order applies to its own civil servants. Private sector employers are being **urged** to mirror the schedule, but it's not a mandate. Most large employers in the Klang Valley are reviewing internally — expect industry-led announcements over the coming weeks rather than a blanket law.
---
q: What if my home electricity bill jumps and wipes out the fuel savings?
a: That's a measurement problem, not a WFH problem. Most people overestimate the electricity bump (aircon during office hours is the biggest swing factor). Track both Petrol and Electricity in Duitful for one full month before you decide. If electricity does eat the savings, the fix is timer + thermostat discipline on WFH days, not abandoning WFH.
---
q: I'm a freelancer with no office to begin with — does any of this apply?
a: Yes. The same playbook applies to discretionary trips: client meetings that could be Zoom, supply runs that could be combined, gym sessions that could be at home twice a week. Pick three recurring trips per week, convert one to remote, log the savings the same way.
---
q: How does this affect EV vs petrol decisions?
a: Less commuting weakens the EV economic case, because the fuel savings are the biggest part of EV total cost of ownership. If you were on the fence about EV at 20,000 km/year of commute, you're firmly off the fence at 12,000 km/year. Track your monthly km in a `Vehicle` category note for the next quarter before any car decision.
---
q: My boss wants the team back full-time despite the energy push. What's the financial case to push back?
a: Run your team's net WFH savings in Duitful for two months — fuel claims, parking reimbursements, office utilities, pantry. Bring the actual ringgit figure to the conversation, not the principle. Concrete savings beat principled arguments every time.
:::
