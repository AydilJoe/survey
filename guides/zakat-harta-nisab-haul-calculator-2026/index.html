Zakat on wealth, without the guesswork.

Published 27 Jul 2026 · Updated 28 Jul 2026

Almost every Malaysian Muslim knows the rate is 2.5%. Far fewer can say what it's 2.5% of, when the twelve-month clock starts, or why their figure looks nothing like their neighbour's. The rules aren't complicated — they're just rarely written down in one place.

Three numbers, not one

Zakat on wealth (zakat harta) is decided by three figures. Get any one wrong and the answer is wrong.

What people usually get wrong

  • Treating nisab as "roughly RM 20k" — it moves with the gold price, weekly
  • Starting the haul from January, rather than from when they crossed nisab
  • Paying 2.5% on the amount above nisab instead of on the whole base

The three inputs that actually decide it

  • Nisab — the wealth floor. Below it, you owe nothing at all.
  • Haul — one lunar year of continuously holding above that floor.
  • The base — your zakatable wealth on the day the haul completes.

The third of those mistakes is the most expensive one, and it runs in your favour by accident: once you're above nisab, zakat is levied on the entire zakatable base, not on the excess. Income tax works in brackets. Zakat does not.

Nisab: a fixed weight, a moving price

85 g

Gold nisab — fixed by fiqh, unchanging

The silver benchmark is 595 g. Only the metal price moves.

The weights are settled: 85 grams of gold (20 mithqal) or 595 grams of silver (200 dirham). What moves is the price per gram, which is why the published nisab changes through the year.

At a gold price of RM 480/g, nisab is RM 40,800. At RM 520/g it's RM 44,200. That's a RM 3,400 swing in the threshold from a price move nobody consulted you about — which is exactly why "I checked last year" is not a method.

The gold benchmark (85 g) is what most Malaysian state authorities publish, and it's the default in nearly every local calculator. It sets a higher threshold, so fewer people are liable. The silver benchmark (595 g) sits materially lower at today's prices — more people become liable, sooner — and some scholars prefer it precisely because it widens the obligation rather than narrowing it.

Both are defensible positions. Pick one, follow your state authority's published figure, and stay consistent — switching benchmarks year to year to land on a smaller bill is not a fiqh position, it's arithmetic shopping.

The haul: a lunar year, from when you crossed

The haul is 354 days, not 365 — a lunar year. And it starts on the day your wealth first crossed nisab, not on 1 January and not on your birthday.

354 days

One lunar year — the haul period

Eleven days shorter than the Gregorian year, which is why your zakat date drifts earlier each year.

The technical condition is that you stay above nisab for the whole period. Dipping below and recovering restarts the clock in the strictest reading — though most state authorities in practice assess on the start and end points rather than daily. If your balance genuinely collapsed mid-year, ask; don't assume.

Once you pay, the haul resets from the payment date. That's why zakat dates creep earlier on the Gregorian calendar year after year.

What counts as zakatable wealth

Generally NOT zakatable

  • Your home, and the car you drive
  • Tools and equipment used to earn a living
  • EPF before withdrawal (most states assess on withdrawal)
  • Jewellery within the customary personal-use allowance
  • Money you are owed but realistically will not recover

Generally zakatable

  • Cash — bank accounts, e-wallets, money at home
  • Savings and fixed deposits (the principal)
  • Gold and silver held as wealth, above personal-use allowances
  • Shares, unit trusts, ASB, Tabung Haji balances
  • Business inventory and receivables you expect to collect

You may deduct immediate liabilities — debts falling due now, not the whole outstanding balance of a 30-year home financing. A common and defensible reading is: deduct the instalments payable within the current period, not the entire facility. Deducting a full RM 400,000 home financing to zero out your zakat is not a reading any authority will accept.

Working an actual example

  1. 1

    Add up the cash and near-cash

    Savings RM 38,000 + current account RM 6,500 + e-wallet RM 500 = RM 45,000. This is the part most people already have a number for.

  2. 2

    Add the investments

    ASB RM 18,000 + unit trusts RM 9,000 = RM 27,000. Running total: RM 72,000. Use the value on the haul date, not what you paid for them.

  3. 3

    Deduct immediate liabilities

    Credit card balance RM 3,200 + this period's financing instalments RM 4,800 = RM 8,000. Zakatable base: RM 64,000. Note what is NOT deducted — the remaining RM 190,000 of the home financing.

  4. 4

    Compare against nisab

    At RM 480/g gold, nisab is RM 40,800. RM 64,000 is comfortably above it, so the obligation is live. Had the base come to RM 38,000, the answer would be zero — not "a smaller amount".

  5. 5

    Take 2.5% of the whole base

    RM 64,000 × 2.5% = RM 1,600. Not 2.5% of the RM 23,200 above nisab, which would have been RM 580. This is the single most common error and it understates by nearly two-thirds.

  6. 6

    Check the haul has actually completed

    If you crossed nisab in March, the haul completes 354 days later — around late February the following year. Paying early is permitted and common; paying on a date you never established is just a habit.

Common questions

Is it 2.5% of everything, or only the part above nisab?

The whole zakatable base, once nisab is met. Nisab is a threshold test, not an exemption band. If your base is RM 64,000 and nisab is RM 40,800, you owe RM 1,600 — not RM 580. This trips up a lot of people because income tax conditions us to think in brackets.

Why does my number differ from my friend's on similar savings?

Usually one of three things: a different nisab benchmark (gold vs silver), a different haul date, or a different treatment of debts. All three are legitimate variations. What isn't legitimate is picking whichever combination gives the smallest number.

Do I pay zakat on my EPF?

Most Malaysian state authorities assess EPF on withdrawal rather than annually, on the reasoning that you don't have full disposal of it while it's locked. Practice does vary by state — this is worth a direct question to yours rather than a rule of thumb.

Can I deduct my home financing?

The instalments falling due in the current period, generally yes. The entire outstanding balance, no. A long-tenure facility would otherwise wipe out the obligation for anyone with a mortgage, which is plainly not the intent.

What if I dip below nisab partway through the year?

Strictly, continuous ownership above nisab is a condition of the haul, and a genuine dip restarts it. In practice most authorities assess the start and end points. If your balance really did collapse mid-year, ask your state authority rather than deciding for yourself in either direction.

The point of tracking it early

Zakat is not a tax you optimise around. But it is a known, recurring, sizeable outflow — and treating it like a surprise is a cash-flow failure, not a religious one. A household with RM 64,000 in zakatable wealth owes RM 1,600 a year, every year, on a date that moves eleven days earlier annually.

Knowing that in month two rather than month twelve is the entire difference between planning for it and scrambling for it.
