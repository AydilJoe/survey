Your PCB already paid the tax. Claim the refund.

Published 28 Apr 2026 · Updated 26 Oct 2026

PCB is taken from your salary before your lifestyle, medical and insurance reliefs are counted. Claim them and the difference comes back. Duitful catches each claimable spend as you log it — and keeps the receipt LHDN will ask for.

What's at stake

RM 34,500

Combined YA 2026 limits of eight reliefs most salaried people can use — lifestyle, sports, medical, two insurance reliefs, EPF, PRS and SSPN

Every RM 1,000 claimed saves RM 60–RM 250 for most salaried incomes, depending on your tax band.

Unless you file a TP1 with your employer, PCB only counts the basic reliefs. Every other relief you can prove pushes your real tax below what was taken — and the difference comes back as a refund.

Your receipts stay yours

Receipt photos and PDFs are encrypted with your passcode and kept on your phone — never uploaded, never in a backup file, readable only after you unlock. LHDN can ask for records for 7 years; Duitful keeps them until you delete the claim.

Common questions

Does Duitful know this year's relief limits?

Yes. The YA 2026 and YA 2027 schedule is built in — including sub-limits like RM 1,000 for dental inside the RM 10,000 medical relief, and RM 300 for sports shoes from YA 2027. It's still an estimate: check LHDN's figures before you file.

How is the refund worked out?

Your gross income minus automatic and claimed reliefs gives chargeable income. Duitful applies the resident tax bands and the RM 400 rebate, takes off any zakat you've logged, and compares the result with the PCB you've paid.

Can one purchase count for two reliefs?

No. Each claim goes to one relief. Duitful suggests the best match and shows how much room is left in each, so you can pick the one you haven't filled yet.

Does any of this go to LHDN?

No. Duitful never sees your data. You type the totals into e-Filing yourself, and the receipts stay on your phone until LHDN asks.
