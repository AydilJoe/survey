"RM 500, until gaji." That was eleven months ago.

Published 30 Jul 2026

It is the most common untracked money in the country — a transfer to a cousin, a sibling, a colleague who left their card at home. No contract, no date either side wrote down, and a slowly growing reluctance to be the person who brings it up. Ten seconds of recording removes most of that.

The problem is the date, not the trust

Nobody forgets RM 500 out of dishonesty. They forget because only one of the two people is thinking about it, and neither wrote a date down. A record with a due date fixes the failure that actually happens.

14 days

When Duitful starts raising a request you never dated

Give a loan a due date and it joins your reminders like any debt due day. Leave the date blank and it surfaces after two weeks — precisely the loan people write off by accident.

Record it in four taps

  1. 1

    Tap "Lent money"

    Person, amount, the date it left, an optional note. RM 500 to Ali on the 3rd. It's a receivable, not a debt — it never touches your avalanche plan, your APR maths or your payoff date.

  2. 2

    Set the due day

    Gaji day, the 15th, whatever you actually agreed. That single field is the difference between a loan you're tracking and a loan you're hoping about.

  3. 3

    Send the request only if you want to

    Optional, always. A QR or a link carries the amount, what it's for and the due date, decoded on their phone. The record and the reminder work perfectly whether or not you ever send it.

  4. 4

    Record repayments as they arrive

    Any amount, any day. Each one books an income row dated to the day it landed, so the money coming back appears in the month it came back.

The reminder is yours, not theirs

What an app that "reminds them" costs you

  • Your cousin gets a notification you didn't write, at a moment you didn't choose
  • They need an account, or an email address you handed over on their behalf
  • A server now knows who owes whom, and how much

How Duitful does it

  • "Ali owes RM 500 — due Friday" appears on YOUR phone
  • You choose the words and the timing, in your own chat
  • Nothing about the loan leaves the device unless you deliberately share it

The borrower is never the channel. That is a design decision, not a missing feature — an app that nags your family on your behalf spends your relationship to save you a text message.

Half now, half next month

Partial repayment is normal between people, so it's the default path rather than an edge case.

RM 300 lands against the RM 500. The row now reads RM 200 of RM 500 left and stays open, with an income row logged for the RM 300 on the day it arrived. The next RM 200 settles it and the reminder disappears. Re-share the request at any point and the new link carries RM 200 — the amount still owed, not the original ask.

Isn't a phone note good enough?

For the amount, yes. For the due date, the partial repayments and the reminder, no — that's the part a note never does. And a note doesn't put the RM 300 back into the month it actually arrived in.

Does the person I lent to see anything?

Only if you send them a request, and only what the share dialog previews before it goes. No sign-up, no notification from Duitful, no server that knows their name. If you never share it, the record is yours alone.

Can I charge interest?

Duitful doesn't model interest on money you lent — that's a relationship decision, not a product feature, and receivables stay entirely out of the debt and avalanche maths. Track the principal and the date.

What if they never pay?

Cancel the person's request and it leaves your owed list without inventing a fake settlement. Nothing gets logged as income that never arrived — the point of an honest tracker is that it stays honest when the news is bad.
