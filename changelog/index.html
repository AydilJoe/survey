What's new in Duitful

Duitful ships as often as it needs to. Every change listed below lands via Vercel on duitful.app — installed PWAs auto-reload on the next open.

August 2026 · v1.29

The same bill, four times, every morning

A reader sent a lock-screen photo with “Utilities (TNB)” on it twice. It should have been on it once, and the reason it wasn’t turns out to be a nice little bug.

  • fixBill reminders no longer stack up. Bills are stored one row per month, and “repeat next month” makes a new row each time — so a bill you’d had since April had four rows by August, and every one of them booked the same reminder. You now get one reminder per bill, showing the most recent amount.
  • fix“Min payment RM 0.00” is gone. A debt with no minimum set now just says “Payment due today”, which is what it meant.
  • fixDark mode no longer flashes cream on launch. The bit of code that reads your theme before the first frame was being blocked by the app’s own security policy — on every single launch, quietly, for months. It runs now.
  • noteInstalled launches are counted, and nothing else is. One cookieless ping when you open Duitful from your home screen, so I have some idea whether anyone is using this. Not a page path, not a tap, not a single figure from your data — and nothing at all in a browser tab or the Android app. The privacy policy spells it out, including how to switch it off.

The iPhone app is real, and it’s an alpha

Not a release — a progress note, because “coming soon” has been sitting on the front page for a while and that’s not very informative.

  • noteThere is a working iOS build. The whole app runs natively on iPhone: passcode, Face ID unlock, receipt scanning, bill splitting, the lot. A home-screen widget is in it too.
  • noteYou can’t install it yet, and that’s the honest part. Putting an app on the App Store or TestFlight needs an Apple Developer account — USD $99 a year. Until that’s paid there is no way to hand you a build, however finished it looks on my desk.
  • noteAlpha means alpha. The known gap is universal links: tapping a shared split link opens Safari instead of jumping into the app. Everything else has been exercised on a real iPhone, but “works on my device” is not the same as tested.
  • noteMeanwhile the web app is a real iPhone app. Open duitful.app/app in Safari, Share → Add to Home Screen, and it runs full-screen and offline with your data encrypted on the device. That is not a stopgap — it’s how most people use Duitful today.

Colour that means something

Duitful looked like a lot of other apps. The frosted panels and the orange-on-cream were doing nothing except being there. Both are gone, and colour now has a job.

  • newAmounts are colour-coded by ringgit note. A RM 3,200 balance carries the RM100 purple; a RM 40 one carries the RM20 orange. It's a scale you already read without thinking, so a list of debts can be ranked before you've read any of it.
  • newFlat surfaces. Every frosted panel is now solid. Text sits on a plain background instead of a blur, which is easier to read and noticeably lighter on cheaper phones — each blurred panel was costing its own drawing layer.
  • noteTerracotta is now the logo colour only. It used to be buttons, links, warnings and the brand all at once, which meant it signalled nothing. Buttons and links are ink; "due soon" is amber; overdue stays red. Three signals that no longer look like each other.
  • fixHeadings and figures got their proper faces. The serif was on every card title, so it emphasised nothing — it's now the wordmark and the one balance on Home. Amounts moved to figures that line up in a column, which is what money actually needs.
August 2026 · v1.25

Knowing which paylater is which

If you're juggling four buy-now-pay-later plans, the hard part isn't the maths — it's working out which row is which before you've read any of them.

  • newInstalment plans carry their provider's colour. Start typing "ato" in the name field and Atome comes up with its colour attached; same for SPayLater, GrabPayLater, Boost, Touch 'n Go and the major banks. A lender that isn't in the list still gets a colour of its own, worked out from the name, so no row ever looks half-finished.
  • new"4 of 12 paid" instead of "8 months left". Same plan, same number, but one of them is ground you've covered and the other is a sentence you're serving. Instalment rows now show a progress bar against the full plan.
  • noteOld plans need one number from you. Duitful never recorded how long a plan originally ran — only what was left — and that can't be worked back out from the balance. Rather than tell you you're at 0 of 12 when you're at 10, those rows keep showing months left until you fill in Total months under ✎.
  • noteThe provider list is bundled, not looked up. Searching a logo service would have meant sending your list of lenders to someone else's server, which is the single most sensitive thing in the app. Matching happens on your device, offline, like everything else.
  • newThe nearest-finished plans now show on Home too. Up to three of them, on the Debts card, sorted by how close they are to done — the bar is the one thing that card couldn't already tell you with a number. If you have no instalment plans, nothing appears.

August 2026 · v1.22

A widget that shows you nothing

Four actions, three sizes, zero figures. The empty space is the feature.

  • newHome-screen widget — Spend, Scan a receipt, Split a bill and Pay debt, one tap from the home screen. Small (2×2 icons), medium (a labelled row) and large (2×2 large buttons). Android in this release; iPhone once the app is on the App Store.
  • noteWhy it shows no balance. Your data is encrypted with a key derived from your passcode, held only while the app is open. A widget is a separate program and cannot decrypt any of it. The only way to put a number on your home screen is to write that number somewhere outside the vault, readable by anyone holding your unlocked phone. We decided that isn't a trade worth making — so the widget has no App Group, no shared storage, no file, no network, and nothing to update.
  • noteIf that trade-off is one you'd want to make for yourself, say so — an opt-in, off-by-default figure is buildable. It just shouldn't be the default, and it shouldn't be silent.

August 2026 · v1.20

Receipts, read properly

Three real Malaysian receipts, three different ways the itemiser failed. One of them was quietly doubling restaurant bills.

  • fixA restaurant bill no longer doubles — a guest check printing NET VALUE (EXCLD TAX) 85.00 had that RM 85 booked as a service charge on top of RM 90.10 of food. Charge keywords are now matched as whole words, restatements of the bill are excluded, and the items are reconciled against the total printed on the receipt: if they already add up, nothing is added on top.
  • fixSupermarket receipts itemise at all — slips that print the item name on one line and the barcode, unit price and line total on the next produced zero items, even from a flawless scan. Both halves were being discarded. They pair up now.
  • fixReadable item names — no more "Naan Cheese + Garlic D 8.10 1" or "0885954430039 LOTUSS CEN". Leading barcodes and trailing price/quantity columns are stripped, while genuinely short name fragments are kept: a hypermarket that truncates "Brokoli China" to "BROKOLI CH" keeps its last word.
  • newQuantities become claimable rows — "3 x Ais Kosong 0.90" is now three rows of RM 0.30, so three people can each take one. Only for small whole counts that divide into clean sen; a tray of 24 eggs stays a single row.
  • new"What did it read?" — every scan can show its working: items found, charges added, the total printed on the receipt, and every line it skipped with the reason. Collapsed by default, and none of it is stored or shared.
  • noteAll three receipts are now fixtures in the test suite, so none of these can come back.

The first second, and the empty screens

An offline-first app was blocking its own first paint on a font CDN. Fixing that turned into a pass over every moment where Duitful had nothing to show you yet.

  • fixNo more white flash on launch — the colours needed for the first frame are now inlined in the page itself, so the app paints its own surface immediately instead of the WebView's default white. Dark mode was the worse case: a bright rectangle on every cold start.

  • newEmpty screens that help — a fresh Debts, Daily, Savings or Reports list now says what belongs there, why it's worth filling, and where there's an obvious next step, offers it. "No debts yet." was true and useless.
  • noteA search with no hits is not an empty state — those keep the compact one-liner with a clear-search link. A full illustrated panel for a typo reads as a dead end.

July 2026 · v1.12

Your coast number

There's a number where retirement stops being a question of saving harder and becomes a question of waiting: the pot that compounds to your target all by itself. Duitful now computes yours — in today's money, on your device, with the assumptions on the table.

  • newRetirement card on Savings — one tap to enable, like zakat. Tell it what you'd spend per month in retirement and it derives your target pot on the 4% rule (or type your own pot to override it).
  • newCoast number today — target ÷ (1 + real return)^years. Your current pot (investments, plus savings goals if you want) is measured against it: past it you're "Coasting ✓"; short of it you see exactly how many ringgit to go.
  • newProjection at retirement — today's pot compounded plus your current monthly saving as an annuity, at a real (after-inflation) return you choose, default 4%. Shown next to what that pot would fund per month.
  • noteEstimates, and labelled as such — everything is in today's money; the 4% rule is a rule of thumb, not a guarantee; a nonsense horizon (retire age below current age) shows "—", never a made-up figure. The whole plan round-trips through CSV backup, and turning the card off keeps your inputs for when you come back.
July 2026 · v1.11

The return your bank statement won't tell you

A holding that "went up RM 2,100" tells you almost nothing if you kept topping it up along the way. Duitful now computes the number that accounts for that: your money-weighted return — and refuses to guess when there isn't enough history to say it honestly.

  • newMoney-weighted return — per holding and for the whole portfolio, annualised since inception. It weighs every top-up, withdrawal and cash dividend at the date it actually happened against today's value, solved numerically rather than approximated. Reinvested dividends are correctly not counted as new money.
  • newPortfolio value over time — a chart in Reports built from every valuation you've recorded, each holding carried forward to every snapshot date. Hand-drawn SVG, no chart library, nothing fetched.
  • newYield on cost + per-account totals — trailing 12-month dividends against what you actually contributed (a truer figure than yield on current value), and your portfolio grouped by account once you hold more than one.
  • noteHonesty rails — under 90 days of history, or when the maths genuinely has no answer (a wiped-out holding), you get "—", never an annualised guess or a clamped endpoint.

Shariah mode — Islamic financing and zakat, free for everyone

Duitful now speaks Islamic finance properly. Track Murabahah, Tawarruq, BBA, AITAB, Ijarah and Musharakah Mutanaqisah facilities with the maths they actually use — fixed profit that never compounds, and an ibra' estimate showing what settling early really saves. Plus a full zakat-on-wealth tracker with nisab and haul. Both are free on every tier, not Pro features.

  • newShariah mode (free) — Settings → Islamic finance. Relabels the app for Islamic contracts: “profit rate” instead of APR, “profit charges” instead of interest, throughout. Toggle it any time; nothing needs a reload, and turning it off leaves your data untouched.
  • newIslamic financing debt type (free) — a new debt kind alongside Standard and Installment, covering Murabahah, Tawarruq, BBA, AITAB, Ijarah and Musharakah Mutanaqisah. Enter the financed amount, total profit and tenure straight off your offer letter. Duitful shows the instalment, the total payable, and the effective rate live as you type — before you sign anything.
  • newIbra' on early settlement — your bank statement shows the outstanding sale price, which includes profit you haven't been charged yet. Duitful shows the outstanding principal — what settling today actually costs — with the ibra' spelled out beside it, so the two numbers reconcile. The payoff simulator models profit that stops accruing once the principal clears, so accelerating a facility finally shows up as real savings instead of no change at all.
  • newPayoff queue understands flat rates — an Islamic facility has no APR, but it isn't free either. Duitful converts the flat profit to an effective annual rate, so a 4.8% flat facility queues at roughly 9.44% — correctly ahead of an 8% card and behind a 15% one. Previously the avalanche planner would have sorted it last on a 0% APR and left you clearing the wrong debt for years.
