## What that means in practice

**You may**, without asking:

- Run, modify and self-host the software
- Distribute your modified version under a different name
- Say truthfully that your project is *"based on Duitful"*, *"a fork of
  Duitful"*, or *"compatible with Duitful"* — nominative use is fine, and we
  will not send you a letter about it

**Please don't**:

- Publish a fork *called* Duitful, or a name close enough to be mistaken for
  it, to any app store or website
- Use the wordmark or logo as your project's own identity
- Imply that a fork is the official build, or is endorsed by, maintained by,
  or connected to this project

The line is simple: **take the code, bring your own name.**

## Why this exists

A personal-finance app is trusted on the strength of its name. If someone
ships a build called "Duitful" that quietly uploads a vault, the damage lands
on every honest user of the real one — and they have no way to tell the
difference at the point of install. The mark is the only thing that keeps
"which one is the real one?" answerable, and it is the reason the code can be
given away safely.
